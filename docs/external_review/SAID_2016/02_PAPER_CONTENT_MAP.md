# Page-by-page content map

This map is deliberately a research index rather than a reproduction of the article.

## Page 1 — Abstract and bibliographic record

- Experimental solar thermal NH3/H2O absorption refrigeration system.
- Focus on chiller component design, ice storage, solar collector field, data acquisition and control.
- Laboratory chiller developed at ITW/University of Stuttgart; demonstration plant at KFUPM.
- Abstract reports COP 0.69 and 10.1 kW at 114/23/−2 °C.
- Abstract also reports 4.5 kW and COP 0.42 at 140/45/−4 °C.
- Article identity and DOI recorded.

## Page 2 — Absorption-system background

- Distinguishes intermittent and continuous absorption systems.
- Describes continuous systems as simultaneous desorption/absorption using a solution circuit with absorber, solution pump, generator and expansion valve.
- Discusses NH3/H2O versus H2O/LiBr.
- Notes limitations of LiBr/H2O at high condensation temperature and sub-zero evaporation, motivating NH3/H2O.
- Cites earlier solar NH3/H2O work including Boudéhenn et al. and Arias-Varela et al.
- Reports earlier NH3/H2O experimental/model performance from the cited literature.

## Page 3 — Main chiller architecture

- Gives the system schematic.
- Chiller includes generator, dephlegmator, condenser, evaporator, absorber, heat exchangers, expansion valves and solution pump.
- Generator produces NH3-rich vapor containing some water.
- Dephlegmator removes water and a small amount of ammonia; nearly pure ammonia vapor continues.
- Condenser liquefies ammonia.
- Refrigerant heat exchanger cools liquid before expansion.
- Two alternating refrigerant expansion valves regulate mass flow/low pressure.
- Evaporator produces cooling; ammonia exits as saturated vapor and is superheated in the refrigerant heat exchanger.
- Weak solution is cooled through the solution heat exchanger and absorber-pre-cooler before expansion.
- Absorber combines weak solution with ammonia vapor.
- Strong solution is pumped and split between solution heat exchanger and dephlegmator paths before returning to the generator.

## Page 4 — Generator assembly

- Compact generator assembly combines generator, internal solution heat exchanger and rectifier.
- Heating coils are at the bottom.
- Heat-recovery coils are in the middle.
- Rectification zone is at the top.
- Four concentric heating coils share a common heating inlet/outlet.
- Solar-heated water passes through the heating coils.
- Heat-recovery coils preheat strong solution.
- Strong solution distributor sits above the recovery section.
- Diaphragm pump is used for solution circulation.
- Expansion valves are electrically operated and PWM-regulated.
- Other chiller components are plate heat exchangers except the generator assembly.

## Page 5 — Rectification, storage and solar field

- Rectification zone contains stainless-steel packing.
- Strong solution receives heat from internal recovery coils.
- Weak solution leaves the generator and passes through transport tubes.
- NH3/H2O vapor rises through the rectification zone.
- Solar field: 14 evacuated tubular CPC-18 collectors.
- Reported maximum heating power: 20 kW at 140 °C.
- Total collector area: 42 m².
- Collector orientation/inclination stated by the paper.
- Ice and cold-water storage are each reported as 0.41 m³ with 40 kWh cold storage per tank.
- Hydraulic unit provides external-circuit pumps, expansion vessels, air drains and measurement/control functions.

## Page 6 — External system and operating modes

- Heat-rejection inlet/outlet temperature is adjusted through heat-rejection fan speed.
- The system uses five operating modes.
- Modes depend on chiller ON/OFF and cooling demand.
- Ice storage and cold-water storage support periods when chiller output and demand differ.
- The fan-coil inlet temperature is used as a switching condition for some storage modes.
- The paper emphasizes intermittent cold delivery to the room even though the absorption chiller itself is a continuous pumped cycle.

## Page 8 — Representative operating day

- Reports successful commissioning/testing in February 2014.
- Section A: heat-rejection temperature around 45 °C, global solar irradiance 700–850 W/m², generator inlet about 140 °C, evaporator outlet about −4 °C.
- Average cooling performance around 4.5 kW and COP 0.42 for that stable condition.
- Later lower heat-rejection temperature produces higher COP.
- At approximately 121 °C generator inlet, 30 °C condenser/absorber inlet and approximately −5 °C evaporator outlet, average cooling performance is 4.8 kW and COP 0.46.
- Data acquisition interval is reported as 30 seconds.
